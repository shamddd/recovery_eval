## 1. Context and Methodological Problem

Across 200 recovery continuations (20 problems $\times$ 2 models $\times$ 5 seeds), zero instances of spontaneous error correction were observed ($k = 0$). 

Reporting $0/200$ creates two major inferential hazards if not rigorously qualified:
1. **The Fallacy of Absolute Non-Existence**: Claiming that "language models cannot self-correct" or that "the recovery rate is zero in the population" conflates a zero-numerator sample statistic with a universal negative guarantee.
2. **Uncalibrated Statistical Assumptions**: Calculating confidence bounds for $k=0$ using standard binomial formulas requires specific independence assumptions that are violated by clustered evaluation designs.

---

## 2. Mathematical Dissection: One-Sided vs. Two-Sided Bounds

Under the exact Clopper-Pearson method for $k=0$ successes out of $N$ trials:
- **One-Sided 95% Confidence Upper Bound** ($\alpha = 0.05$):
  $$(1 - p_U)^N = 0.05 \implies p_U = 1 - 0.05^{1/N}$$
- **Two-Sided 95% Confidence Interval Upper Endpoint** ($\alpha/2 = 0.025$ in the upper tail):
  $$(1 - p_{U, \text{two-sided}})^N = 0.025 \implies p_{U, \text{two-sided}} = 1 - 0.025^{1/N}$$

### Exact Mathematical Comparison Across Aggregation Tiers:

| Aggregation Tier | Effective Sample ($N$) | Observed Successes ($k$) | One-Sided 95% Upper Bound | Two-Sided 95% Confidence Interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rollout Level (Total)** | $n = 200$ | $k = 0$ | **1.49%** ($p_U = 0.01487$) | **$[0.00\%, 1.83\%]$** ($p_U = 0.01827$) |
| **Model Level (Base / Instruct)** | $n = 100$ | $k = 0$ | **2.95%** ($p_U = 0.02951$) | **$[0.00\%, 3.62\%]$** ($p_U = 0.03622$) |
| **Problem Cluster Level (Sensitivity)** | $C = 20$ | $k = 0$ | **13.91%** ($p_U = 0.13911$) | **$[0.00\%, 16.84\%]$** ($p_U = 0.16843$) |

*Audit Finding:* The previously reported values of **1.49%** ($n=200$) and **13.91%** ($C=20$) are strictly **one-sided 95% confidence upper bounds**. In earlier drafts, they were occasionally labeled as "endpoints of a 95% confidence interval" without clarifying the tail probability. The manuscript must explicitly label them as one-sided 95% upper bounds while noting the corresponding two-sided upper endpoints (1.83% and 16.84%).

## 3. Disclosing Underlying Assumptions and Limitations

### A. The Rollout-Level Calculation ($N = 200 \implies 1.49\%$)
- **Required Assumption**: Assumes 200 independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) Bernoulli trials.
- **Violation in Experimental Design**: The 200 rollouts are generated by repeating 5 stochastic seeds across only 20 problem templates. Because the prompt prefixes within each problem share identical numerical values and grammatical templates, within-problem outcomes are positively correlated.
- **Scientific Caveat**: The 1.49% bound cannot be interpreted as an unconditional population guarantee for language models on arbitrary reasoning tasks.

### B. The Problem-Cluster Calculation ($C = 20 \implies 13.91\%$)
- **Nature of the Calculation**: This is a **sensitivity calculation** evaluating what the bound would be if each problem cluster were treated as a single binary Bernoulli unit ($k=0$ successes across 20 independent problem types).
- **Scientific Caveat**: It provides an informative upper sensitivity bound ($13.91\%$ one-sided, $16.84\%$ two-sided), but does not constitute a formal assumption-free cluster-robust confidence bound.
