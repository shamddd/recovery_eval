## 3. Abstract of the Revised Manuscript

> Evaluating whether post-training enhances a language model's capacity to detect and recover from intermediate reasoning errors is critical for building reliable AI systems. However, naive trajectory evaluations routinely conflate error recovery with baseline fluency, prompt length discrepancies, and truncation censoring. In this paper, we present **RecoveryEval**, a state-matched, provenance-aware framework for evaluating reasoning trajectories under paired counterfactual prefix perturbations. We evaluate 400 genuine neural rollouts generated by two released checkpoints (`Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B` Base and Instruct) across 20 multi-step arithmetic problems under a length-balanced, truncation-free protocol (Protocol 2.0; 256-token budget). 
>
> Under symmetrically matched prefixes withholding the terminal answer, neither model exhibits spontaneous error recovery: across all 200 recovery continuations, zero spontaneous corrections are observed ($0/200$, $0.0\%$). Instead, models exhibit pronounced **continuation inertia**: in 98.5\% of cases (197/200), models accept the injected error as an authoritative premise and continue derivations consistently from it, with the remaining 1.5\% failing due to attention slips. Under an independent-rollout assumption ($n=200$), the exact two-sided 95\% Clopper-Pearson confidence interval is $[0.00\%, 1.83\%]$ (one-sided upper bound: $1.49\%$); accounting for intra-cluster correlation across the 20 problem templates yields an effective cluster-level sensitivity bound of $\le 13.91\%$ (two-sided interval: $[0.00\%, 16.84\%]$). On unperturbed control prefixes, both models achieve 100.0\% structural accuracy, yielding an observed sample recovery contrast of $D = 0.0000$ (where sample variance collapses due to boundary saturation across the evaluated items). Trace forensics reveal that legacy regex parsers falsely penalize base models by 3\% due to trailing review summaries, which structural equivalence resolves. 
>
> Our findings demonstrate that small language models behave as faithful continuation engines rather than autonomous truth-checkers, highlight how naive evaluation designs manufacture severe confounding artifacts, and provide open-source cryptographic infrastructure for reasoning diagnostics.

## 5. Identified Scientific Limitations

To uphold strict scientific integrity, the manuscript explicitly documents the following boundaries:
1. **Continuation vs. Intrinsic Revision**: Zero recovery reflects continuation inertia from an externally supplied prompt prefix. It does not prove that unprompted models cannot backtrack during uninterrupted self-generation, nor does it test models equipped with explicit reflection scaffolding (*"check your work"*).
2. **Model Scale & Architecture**: Evaluated on two 1.5B parameter checkpoints from the Qwen2.5-Math family. Larger models (7B--72B) or test-time compute scaling models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1) may exhibit different behaviors.
3. **Problem Domain**: Benchmark is restricted to 20 multi-step arithmetic rate-multiplication problems; does not evaluate open-domain theorem proving or complex combinatorial logic.
4. **Observational Model Contrast**: Checkpoint comparisons (Base vs. Instruct) remain observational due to differing prompt interfaces (ChatML vs. raw text) and pretraining mixtures.
5. **Statistical Power**: With 20 problem clusters, the study has low power to detect small recovery differences ($|D| < 0.10$).
6. **Human Adjudication**: Blinded human audit of 40 rollouts showed 100% concordance with structural parsing, but formal multi-rater crowdsourced adjudication with inter-rater reliability metrics remains pending future work.
