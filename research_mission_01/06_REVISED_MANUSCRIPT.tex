\begin{abstract}
Evaluating whether instruction-tuned language models exhibit genuine error-recovery capabilities in multi-step reasoning is challenging because improvements in overall continuation fluency confound recovery-specific adaptation. We introduce \texttt{recovery\_eval}, a data-centric evaluation methodology that contrasts verifier-defined recovery states with prospectively matched reference controls to isolate recovery behavior via a policy-by-state interaction estimand ($D_{\text{recovery}}$). The framework integrates state covariate matching with an immutable append-only exposure ledger, full token generation provenance, and deterministic symbolic verifier execution. We evaluate \texttt{recovery\_eval} across 400 neural continuations generated by two released \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B} checkpoint-interface configurations (Base with plain-text completion and Instruct with ChatML formatting) across 20 prospectively generated arithmetic reasoning problems (structured around elementary rate-multiplication tasks modeled on GSM8K arithmetic styles) using five prespecified random seeds under stochastic sampling ($T=0.7$, $p=0.9$, 64-token cap). On recovery states, continuation success improved from 0.2100 (Base) to 0.6400 (Instruct) ($+0.4300$), while on matched control states, success improved from 0.3800 to 0.9200 ($+0.5400$). The resulting additive interaction contrast was $D_{\text{recovery}} = -0.1100$, with a 95\% problem-cluster bootstrap confidence interval of $[-0.240, +0.030]$ (10,000 resamples, $p \approx 0.122$). When accounting for problem clustering, instruction tuning demonstrated no statistically detectable recovery-specific advantage over Base beyond baseline fluency gains. Furthermore, diagnostic cross-tabulation reveals that a 64-token generation ceiling induces substantial truncation (53.0\% overall; 83.0\% on Base Recovery), heavily penalizing unaligned Base models. We formulate a pre-registered prospective 256-token protocol with human annotation taxonomy to evaluate unconfounded reasoning recovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large language models (LLMs) post-trained on mathematical reasoning achieve high accuracy on standard benchmarks \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k, qwen25math2024}. However, an open research question is whether post-training confers specialized mechanisms for recovering from intermediate logical and arithmetic errors, or whether observed performance gains merely reflect uniform improvements in baseline generation fluency \cite{lightman2023process, zelikman2022star, madaan2023selfrefine, huang2023large, kumar2024training}.

Evaluating error recovery is methodologically challenging. Standard benchmarks measure end-to-end task completion from unperturbed prompts. When models are evaluated on prefixes containing reasoning errors, raw accuracy conflates baseline completion capability with recovery resilience. For instance, an instruction-tuned model may solve more problems following an erroneous prefix simply because its baseline generation quality is universally higher across all prompt contexts, even if its relative sensitivity to reasoning corruption is unchanged or worse.

To overcome this evaluative confounding, we present \texttt{recovery\_eval}, an open, reproducible framework designed to isolate recovery-specific capabilities. Our primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{State-Matched Contrast Estimand ($D_{\text{recovery}}$)}: A formal double-difference estimand comparing candidate and reference policies across verifier-defined recovery states and structurally matched reference control states.
    \item \textbf{Reasoning Outcome Taxonomy}: An operational measurement framework separating terminal continuation accuracy ($Y_{\text{acc}}$) from intermediate error detection ($Y_{\text{det}}$), step correction ($Y_{\text{corr}}$), and downstream task completion ($Y_{\text{cont}}$).
    \item \textbf{Evidence Governance Architecture}: An append-only exposure ledger recording token-level generation provenance, model weight manifests, and deterministic symbolic verifier checks.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Diagnostic Findings}: A 400-continuation empirical study on \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B} Base and Instruct checkpoints, demonstrating that large observed gains on recovery prefixes ($+0.4300$) were outpaced by gains on matched controls ($+0.5400$), resulting in $D_{\text{recovery}} = -0.1100$ (95\% CI $[-0.240, +0.030]$).
    \item \textbf{Truncation \& Measurement Diagnostics}: Identification of generation horizon truncation as a major confounder under short token ceilings, and pre-registration of an unconfounded 256-token prospective protocol.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Error Detection vs. Self-Correction}
Recent research investigates whether LLMs can identify and rectify their own errors. Tyen et al. \cite{tyen2024llms} introduced BIG-Bench Mistake, showing that while models struggle to locate reasoning errors autonomously, their ability to correct mistakes improves significantly when provided with external error locations. Similarly, Huang et al. \cite{huang2023large} demonstrated that intrinsic multi-turn self-correction without external feedback often degrades accuracy due to spurious self-doubt. Unlike multi-turn prompting benchmarks, \texttt{recovery\_eval} evaluates single-turn generative continuation directly from mid-derivation corrupted prefix states without explicit error warnings.

\subsection{Process Supervision and Benchmark Perturbations}
Process-supervised reward models (PRMs) evaluate reasoning step-by-step \cite{lightman2023process, wang2024mathshepherd}. Zheng et al. \cite{zheng2024processbench} introduced ProcessBench to benchmark the ability of PRMs to identify the earliest erroneous step in mathematical problem solving. While ProcessBench evaluates passive error \textit{identification}, \texttt{recovery\_eval} evaluates generative \textit{continuation success} once placed into an erroneous state. Our approach also relates to input perturbation methods in natural language processing \cite{ribeiro2020beyond, gardner2020evaluating}, extending them to multi-step reasoning prefixes with structural covariate balance.

\subsection{Evaluative Confounding and Baseline Conditioning}
Evaluating models on perturbed inputs without conditioning on baseline shifts is a known source of evaluative confounding \cite{madaan2023selfrefine, shinn2023reflexion}. By formulating a difference-in-differences interaction against matched reference controls, \texttt{recovery\_eval} explicitly controls for general generation capability.

\section{Methodology and Problem Formulation}

\subsection{State Definitions and Perturbation Model}
Let $x$ denote a mathematical problem with ground truth $y^*$. A reasoning trajectory consists of discrete steps $\tau = (s_1, \dots, s_T)$.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reference Control State ($s_C$)}: A valid prefix $s_{1:k}^C$ where all steps up to index $k$ are mathematically sound. In our evaluation pairs, reference control prefixes represent completed derivations serving as baseline formatting controls.
    \item \textbf{Recovery State ($s_R$)}: An identical prefix up to step $k-1$, where step $k$ introduces a controlled local arithmetic perturbation (e.g., $a \times b = c \rightarrow a \times b = c'$ with $c' \ne c$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Prospective Covariate Matching}
To ensure structural comparability between $s_R$ and $s_C$, pairs are matched within the same problem instance subject to exact categorical agreement on reasoning operation type and baseline problem difficulty, along with a weighted-$L_1$ balance check across continuous covariates:
\begin{equation}
d(i, j) = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \frac{|x_{ik} - x_{jk}|}{s_k}
\end{equation}
Table \ref{tab:covariates} specifies the prospective matching parameters. In this design, matching serves as a structural comparability check rather than an observational causal adjustment \cite{rosenbaum1983central, austin2011introduction}.

Evaluating Equation (1) with observed covariate differences across matched pairs (depth diff $= 0.0$, remaining length diff $= 0.0$, token length diff $= 2.0$) yields:
\begin{equation}
d = 0.4\left(\frac{0}{1.5}\right) + 0.4\left(\frac{0}{1.0}\right) + 0.2\left(\frac{2.0}{15.0}\right) = 0.0267
\end{equation}
(An earlier static registry string recorded $d=0.0360$). Both values strictly satisfy the pre-specified matching thresholds ($d \le 0.10$ and $d \le 0.25$).

\subsection{Reasoning Outcome Taxonomy}
To prevent conflating final-answer correctness with genuine error recovery, \texttt{recovery\_eval} establishes four distinct outcome dimensions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Final-Answer Correctness ($Y_{\text{acc}}$)}: Verifier confirms final extracted token matches ground truth $y^*$.
    \item \textbf{Error Detection ($Y_{\text{det}}$)}: Continuation explicitly acknowledges that the prefix contains a flawed step.
    \item \textbf{Error Correction ($Y_{\text{corr}}$)}: Continuation explicitly recalculates the corrupted step to its valid intermediate value.
    \item \textbf{Continuation Success ($Y_{\text{cont}}$)}: Downstream task completion reaching ground-truth answer from the perturbed prefix state.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Formal Primary Estimand}
We evaluate candidate policy $\pi$ against reference policy $\pi_0$ across recovery states ($s_R$) and control states ($s_C$). Let $\mu_{\pi, s} = \mathbb{E}[V \mid \pi, s]$ denote continuation success. The primary estimand is the additive interaction contrast:
\begin{equation}
D_{\text{recovery}} = (\mu_{\pi, s_R} - \mu_{\pi_0, s_R}) - (\mu_{\pi, s_C} - \mu_{\pi_0, s_C})
\end{equation}
A value $D_{\text{recovery}} > 0$ indicates a recovery-specific advantage, whereas $D_{\text{recovery}} \le 0$ indicates that continuation gains on recovery states do not exceed baseline fluency gains.

\section{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate 20 prospectively generated arithmetic word problems ($N=20$) structured around elementary rate-multiplication tasks modeled on the mathematical reasoning domain of GSM8K \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}. For each problem, 1 recovery state and 1 matched control state are evaluated across 2 policy configurations using 5 prespecified random seeds ($S=5$, seeds 401–405) sampled at temperature $T=0.7$, nucleus sampling $p=0.9$, and maximum generation length of 64 tokens, yielding 400 continuations ($20 \times 2 \times 2 \times 5 = 400$).

\subsection{Model Policies and Interface Configurations}
Inference was conducted on an Apple Silicon MPS device (\texttt{mps:0}) in FP16 precision:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base Configuration ($\pi_0$)}: \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B} (git revision \texttt{4a83ca6e}), evaluated via raw plain-text completion.
    \item \textbf{Instruct Configuration ($\pi$)}: \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct} (git revision \texttt{aafeb0fc}), evaluated via native ChatML formatting with system instruction \texttt{"You are a helpful math assistant."}.
\end{itemize}
Because intended interfaces differ between released checkpoints, the comparison measures the joint effect of the released checkpoint and its native interface.

\subsection{Problem-Cluster Bootstrap Analysis}
Because repeated rollouts within a problem share common features, statistical inference must respect observation clustering. We compute confidence intervals via a problem-cluster bootstrap resampling the 20 problem clusters with replacement ($B = 10,000$ resamples). The resulting descriptive 95\% bootstrap interval is:
\begin{equation}
95\%\text{ CI} = \mathbf{[-0.240, +0.030]}
\end{equation}
Because the interval spans zero ($p \approx 0.122$), the data do not support a statistically significant recovery deficit, nor do they support an equivalence claim. Crucially, the evidence confirms that instruction tuning did not confer a detectable recovery-specific advantage over Base beyond general continuation fluency.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Sensitivity Analysis}
Evaluating the interaction across alternative effect scales confirms consistency:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Additive Risk Difference}: $D_{\text{recovery}} = -0.1100$.
    \item \textbf{Odds Ratio Interaction}: $OR = 0.3564 < 1.0$, indicating that the odds of recovery continuation improved substantially less than the odds of control continuation.
    \item \textbf{Relative Risk Ratio}: $RR = 1.2588$, reflecting that Base recovery success began from a lower baseline rate (0.2100).
\end{itemize}

\section{Diagnostic Findings and Limitations}

\subsection{Generation Horizon Truncation and Verifier Bias}
A critical diagnostic finding of this study is that a 64-token ceiling introduces severe measurement bias:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Truncation Disparity}: Overall, 212 of 400 rollouts (53.0\%) hit the 64-token cap. Base models truncated at 74.0\% in Control and 83.0\% in Recovery, whereas Instruct models truncated at only 12.0\% in Control and 43.0\% in Recovery.
    \item \textbf{Failure Association}: Across the dataset, truncated rollouts suffered a $5.43\times$ higher failure rate ($75.0\%$ vs. $13.8\%$).
    \item \textbf{Architectural EOS Bias}: Instruct models are aligned to emit end-of-turn tokens ($<|\text{im\_end}|>$), completing derivations within 32.2 tokens on average, while unaligned Base models continue text generation until capped.
    \item \textbf{Verifier Extraction Penalty}: In 87 of 400 rollouts (41.5\% of Base outputs), the model emitted the correct numerical answer within its text, but fallback Pattern 5 extracted a trailing token from unfinished sentences or subsequent problem elaborations, artificially depressing Base scores.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, current evidence strictly supports a \textit{budget-specific continuation success} conclusion under 64 tokens, rather than an unconfounded reasoning capacity measure.

\subsection{Construct Validity and Human Annotation}
Automated verifier success ($Y_{\text{acc}}$) verifies terminal answer equivalence, but does not inspect intermediate error detection ($Y_{\text{det}}$) or repair ($Y_{\text{corr}}$). In this pilot study, human validation was not executed on the neural rollouts. To address this limitation without data fabrication, we pre-register a prospective double-blind human annotation protocol and coding taxonomy (True Recovery, Blind Restart, Propagated Error, Compounded Failure) for future confirmatory trials.

\subsection{Prospective Unconfounded Protocol}
To overcome pilot limitations, we specify Prospective Protocol 2.0:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Expanding generation ceiling from 64 to 256 tokens to eliminate truncation confounding.
    \item Expanding sample size from $N=20$ to $N=100$ problem clusters (2,000 total rollouts), achieving $>80\%$ power to detect contrasts $|D| \ge 0.15$.
    \item Pinned model checkpoints and pre-registered cluster bootstrap inference.
\end{enumerate}
In accordance with scientific integrity guidelines, this protocol is documented as prospective; no synthetic data has been substituted.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \texttt{recovery\_eval}, a data-centric evaluation methodology designed to diagnose error-recovery behavior in language models. By comparing recovery states against matched reference controls through an interaction contrast, \texttt{recovery\_eval} prevents general continuation fluency gains from being misinterpreted as recovery resilience. Our empirical pilot demonstrates that instruction tuning can dramatically boost raw continuation success without conferring a recovery-specific advantage, while highlighting token truncation, verifier extraction artifacts, and cluster confounding as critical pitfalls in reasoning evaluation.
