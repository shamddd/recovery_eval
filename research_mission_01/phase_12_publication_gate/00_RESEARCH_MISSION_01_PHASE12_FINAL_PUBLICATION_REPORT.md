   $$\hat{D}_{\text{structural}} = 0.0000 \quad (SE_{\text{empirical}} = 0.0000)$$

## Section C: Definitive Resolution of Zero-Recovery Uncertainty

A critical methodological objection addressed in Phase XII is whether observing 0 recoveries among 200 rollouts implies that language models have zero capability to self-correct.

### 1. Clopper-Pearson Exact Confidence Bounds
Using exact inversion of the cumulative binomial distribution (the Clopper-Pearson interval):
$$p_U = 1 - (1 - \alpha)^{1/n}$$

We evaluated the exact upper bounds across three aggregation tiers at confidence level $1 - \alpha = 0.95$:

| Aggregation Tier | Effective Sample Size ($N$) | Observed Successes ($k$) | Point Estimate | Exact 95% Confidence Interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rollout Level (Total)** | $n = 200$ | $k = 0$ | $0.00\%$ | **$[0.00\%, 1.49\%]$** |
| **Model Level (Base / Instruct)** | $n = 100$ | $k = 0$ | $0.00\%$ | **$[0.00\%, 2.95\%]$** |
| **Problem Cluster Level** | **$C = 20$** | **$k = 0$** | **$0.00\%$** | **$[0.00\%, 13.91\%]$** |

### 2. Inferential Conclusion
- The naive rollout-level bound ($1.49\%$) assumes that each rollout represents an independent Bernoulli trial. However, because each problem template is evaluated under 5 stochastic seeds, intra-cluster correlation inflates the effective variance.
- When clustered by problem template ($C=20$), the conservative upper bound is **13.91\%**.
- Therefore, the manuscript refrains from asserting that recovery probability is universally zero; rather, it reports: *"No successful recovery was observed among 200 continuations under the evaluated protocol, placing an upper 95% confidence bound of 1.49% at the rollout level and 13.91% at the problem cluster level."*
