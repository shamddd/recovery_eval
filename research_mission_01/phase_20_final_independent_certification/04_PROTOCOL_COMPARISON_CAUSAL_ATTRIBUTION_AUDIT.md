## 1. Executive Summary

This report audits the historical comparison between **Protocol 1.0** (the preliminary exploratory runs conducted in Phases I–IV) and **Protocol 2.0** (the rigorous, balanced experimental protocol conducted in Phases V–XIV).

In earlier drafts of the manuscript, the text asserted that the statistically significant interaction effect observed in Protocol 1.0 (where the instruct model appeared to show higher recovery than the base model) was specifically caused by answer leakage, token truncation, and heuristic verifier misclassifications.

While these historical defects were indeed documented and verified, **multiple methodological components changed simultaneously between Protocol 1.0 and Protocol 2.0**. Because no full combinatorial ablation was conducted across all confounding variables, asserting that any single defect uniquely manufactured the historical result oversteps the available empirical evidence.

The manuscript has been corrected to use appropriately bounded scientific language:
> *"The historical interaction was not reproduced under the revised evaluation protocol."*

## 2. Multi-Component Protocol Comparison Matrix

The table below catalogs every dimension that varied between Protocol 1.0 and Protocol 2.0:

| Dimension | Protocol 1.0 (Historical Baseline) | Protocol 2.0 (Revised Protocol) | Confounding Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Benchmark Problems** | Unverified public GSM8K subsets | 20 novel synthetic GSM-fresh problems | Possible memorization in Protocol 1.0 vs zero memorization in 2.0 |
| **Prefix Symmetry** | Asymmetrical lengths; variable token boundaries | Token-matched, character-balanced pre-cut prefixes ($\Delta_{\text{chars}} = 0$) | Asymmetric prefix lengths can bias transformer positional encodings |

| **Verifier Pipeline** | Naive trailing-integer heuristic | Multi-layer verifier (LaTeX boxed, semantic, normalizer) | 3 false negatives detected in heuristic parser |
| **Random Seeds** | Exploratory seed sets | Frozen 5-seed matrix ($401, 402, 403, 404, 405$) | Finite sampling variability |
| **Statistical Model** | Unpaired observational regression | Balanced paired factorial contrast with exact non-parametric bounds | Clustered variance underestimation |

## 3. Causal Attribution Limitations

Because at least **nine distinct methodological dimensions** were altered simultaneously during the transition to Protocol 2.0:
1. **Identifiability Failure**: It is statistically impossible to isolate the exact marginal contribution of formula leakage versus prompt formatting versus verifier misclassification using only the endpoints.
2. **Plausibility vs. Proof**: The documented defects in Protocol 1.0 represent **plausible, demonstrated vulnerabilities** that compromised its validity, but claiming that defect $A$ produced $X\%$ of the interaction while defect $B$ produced $Y\%$ is unsupportable without running 512 expensive factorial ablation experiments.
3. **Budgetary Constraint**: In accordance with the project mandate of strictly **USD $0.00 additional cloud expenditure**, running retrospective ablations on paid GPUs is prohibited.

## 4. Textual Remediation in Manuscript

The manuscript has been systematically amended:

- **Section 1 (Introduction)**:
  *Old*: "We prove that the previously reported interaction between model type and recovery behavior was a false positive generated by formula leakage, truncation, and heuristic verifier errors."  
  *Remediated*: "We demonstrate that the previously reported interaction between model type and recovery behavior was not reproduced under our revised, balanced evaluation protocol, which remediates several identified methodological vulnerabilities including token truncation, prompt formatting errors, and verifier misclassifications."

- **Section 5.3 (Historical Comparison)**:
  *Old*: "The historical interaction was an artifact manufactured by formula leakage."  
  *Remediated*: "The historical interaction was not reproduced under the revised evaluation protocol. While formula leakage, token truncation, and parser inaccuracies represent demonstrated flaws in earlier setups, the joint alteration of multiple protocol components prevents isolating the individual causal contribution of each historical factor."

The revised language accurately reflects the empirical evidence. The manuscript presents an honest, defensible account of protocol differences without speculative causal overreach.
