### arXiv Web-Form Optimized Abstract (1,684 characters, 212 words):
```
Evaluating whether post-training enhances a language model's ability to detect and recover from intermediate reasoning errors is critical for reliable artificial intelligence. However, standard trajectory evaluations frequently conflate recovery ability with baseline task accuracy, prompt length disparities, and truncation censoring. In this paper, we present RecoveryEval, a state-matched, provenance-governed evaluation framework assessing autoregressive reasoning trajectories under paired counterfactual prefix perturbations. We evaluate 400 genuine neural rollouts generated by Qwen2.5-Math-7B (Base and Instruct) across 20 multi-step arithmetic problems under a length-balanced, truncation-free protocol (Protocol 2.0; 256-token budget).

Under symmetrically matched prefixes that withhold the terminal answer, neither model exhibits spontaneous error recovery: across all 200 recovery continuations, zero spontaneous corrections occur (0/200, 0.0%). Instead, models exhibit pronounced continuation inertia: in 98.5% of rollouts (197/200), models treat the injected error as an authoritative premise and continue calculations consistently from that corrupted value. Under an independent-rollout assumption, the exact two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson confidence interval is [0.00%, 1.83%]; cluster sensitivity analysis yields a bound of <= 13.91% for problem-level recovery probability. Dual-verifier forensics show that legacy regex parsers falsely penalize base models by 3% due to review summaries. Our findings demonstrate that small language models behave as faithful continuation engines rather than autonomous truth-checkers.
```
